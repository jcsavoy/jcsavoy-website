Meet Faith

Faith is warm, quick to laugh, and the kind of person who makes strangers feel welcome without trying. She's gifted — the girl who could play back any song after hearing it once, whose voice lifts even a simple melody — but Nashville left her wary of the hustle, gun-shy about big commitments, and drifting on purpose while she figures out what comes next. What she's quietly searching for is the part of music she fell in love with as a kid: not the industry, not the charts, but the human side, the part that connects people. She just has to remember she's allowed to want it.

The look: Warm and quick to smile, with long dark hair she usually wears in a braid and hazel eyes. She's just as at home in a barn coat and boots as she ever was onstage — the relaxed confidence of a woman who belongs to this land as much as it belongs to her.

The heart of her story

Faith chased music all the way to Nashville and made it — right up until the grind, the endless hustle, and writing songs for people who treated music like a commodity nearly killed the thing she loved most. She came home with a guitar, a suitcase, and no plan. Christmas Carol Cowgirl is the story of a woman rediscovering why she fell for music in the first place, with a little help from a guarded music-shop owner and a town that never forgot her.
