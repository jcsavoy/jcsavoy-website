Meet Zane

Zane is a man of few words and decisive action — quiet confidence, not shyness. He'll get tongue-tied around people he doesn't know, but when something matters, he's direct and sure. He loves the land, the animals, and the deep satisfaction of building something with his own two hands. There's a stubborn pride under all that steadiness, though: when Colt handed down trust funds, Zane got money but no land, and he's been saving every dollar toward a ranch of his own rather than ask his family for a single acre. He'd sooner fail on his own terms than succeed on anyone's help — the one belief his heart is about to put to the test.

The look: Tall and strong through the shoulders — the build of a man who works with his hands and means to keep doing it. Dark hair kept short, a trimmed beard, and the settled, easy way of someone more at home leaning on a fence rail than making conversation. Dresses simple: work jacket, open collar, boots that have earned their scuffs.

The heart of his story

Years ago, when Raven confided that her grandmother's will would force her to marry by thirty or lose the family law practice, Zane did the most Zane thing imaginable: he offered, plainly, to marry her himself if it ever came to that. She turned him down — she wanted real love, not convenience — and they both carried that conversation for over a decade, neither of them marrying anyone else. Now Raven's back in Cottonwood Ridge with the deadline bearing down. Trust Me, Cowboy is the story of two old friends finding out whether the thing that's always been between them is worth trusting.
