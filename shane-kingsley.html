Meet Shane

Shane is the one who counts — times, distances, head of stock, the exact minute he drove through town. His mind builds order out of chaos, which is exactly the skill that made him a tech founder in Austin, and exactly the thing he can't stop doing now that the company's gone and he's home running his father's elite winter sale instead of a life. He's steady, precise, and quietly reeling from a failure he hasn't made peace with. The old reflex was to hold his cards close and arrange outcomes from behind the curtain; the harder, newer one is to lay everything on the table and let people see the whole board. What he hasn't figured out yet is what to do when the thing that matters most can't be measured.

The look: Tall and lean, with dark hair kept short and the clean, put-together look of a man who spent more recent years behind a desk than in a saddle. Clean-shaven, an easy half-smile, dressed a step sharper than the hands around him — a sherpa-lined jacket over a dark shirt, dark denim, good boots. Notably, no cowboy hat: he's the Kingsley who still looks a little like he wandered in from somewhere else.

The heart of his story

Shane came back to Cottonwood Ridge to run the numbers on his father's biggest horse sale of the year — a job he's been doing instead of building something of his own. Then the sale horses start turning up hurt in ways that shouldn't be happening, and the one person who can read what's really wrong is a traveling equine massage therapist who reads a body like a ledger he never authorized. Hold Still, Cowboy is the story of a man who trusts data over instinct, learning to want something he can't quantify — and to act, for once, without a backup plan.
